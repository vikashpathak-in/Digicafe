Vendor Master Data Controls: GSTIN, Bank Details and Excel Checks

Published 28 November 2026 · 6 min read

A vendor master is used by accounts payable, procurement, tax review and payment teams. If the supplier name, GSTIN, address or bank details are incomplete or outdated, the same error can travel into purchase entries, reconciliations and payments. A controlled onboarding process helps the team catch issues before the first transaction.

This guide sets out a practical review workflow for small finance teams. It is an operational checklist, not a substitute for the organisation's approval policy or professional tax advice.

1. Decide which fields are required

Use one standard vendor form rather than collecting details in different email formats. Depending on the type of supplier and your organisation's policy, the record may include:

  • Unique vendor code and legal business name
  • Trade name, where different from the legal name
  • GSTIN and PAN, where applicable and required
  • Registered or principal business address and state
  • Official contact details and the business contact person
  • Beneficiary name, bank account number and IFSC code
  • Payment terms, currency, vendor category and tax-treatment notes
  • Supporting-document references, created-by details and approval status

Keep bank information restricted to employees who need it. In reports shared widely, mask account numbers and show only the minimum information required for the task.

2. Verify the GST registration details

When a supplier provides a GSTIN, use the GST Portal's Search Taxpayer facility to compare the registration details with the information supplied by the vendor. The portal manual explains that a GSTIN/UIN search can display details such as the legal name, trade name, registration date, business constitution and principal place of business.

Record the date of verification and the result in the vendor file. If the legal name, trade name, state or registration status does not match the documents received, stop the onboarding workflow and ask for clarification. A GSTIN search does not verify ownership of a bank account, prove that an invoice is genuine or decide whether a particular input tax credit is eligible.

3. Treat bank-detail changes as a separate control

A request to change a supplier's bank account should not be processed solely because an email or letter has arrived. Use a known contact channel already held on file to confirm the request. Do not rely only on the phone number or reply address included in the change request itself.

  • Obtain the revised bank proof required by your policy.
  • Compare the beneficiary name and account details with the approved documents.
  • Have a second authorised person review the change.
  • Record who requested, checked and approved the change, along with the effective date.
  • Keep the previous details in the audit trail rather than silently overwriting the history.

4. Use Excel to flag records for review

Assume the vendor code is in column A and the GSTIN is in column C, with data from row 2 to row 500. Add a review column and use this formula to flag repeated GSTIN values:

=IF(C2="","Check requirement",IF(COUNTIF($C$2:$C$500,C2)>1,"Review duplicate","OK"))

If vendor codes must be unique, a separate check can be used in another column:

=IF(A2="","Missing vendor code",IF(COUNTIF($A$2:$A$500,A2)>1,"Duplicate code","OK"))

Important: a repeated value is a review flag, not proof that a record should be deleted. A supplier may have more than one registration or vendor relationship. Compare the supporting documents and business context before merging or blocking records. Microsoft explains how COUNTIF counts cells that meet a criterion and how removing duplicates can permanently delete rows.

5. Keep an exception log

Do not leave a mismatch in an email thread with no owner. Maintain a simple exception tracker with these columns:

  • Vendor code and supplier name
  • Issue type: GSTIN, legal name, address, bank detail or duplicate
  • Evidence requested and evidence received
  • Action owner and target date
  • Approval status and approver
  • Closure date and final remarks

Use clear statuses such as Pending documents, Under review, Approved and Rejected. Do not activate a vendor or release a payment while a mandatory control remains unresolved.

6. Review the vendor master periodically

On a scheduled basis, review inactive vendors, repeated GSTINs, incomplete records, recent bank changes and suppliers whose registration details need rechecking. The review frequency should follow your transaction volume, risk assessment and internal policy.

DigiCafe control note

A good vendor master is not just a list of names. It is a controlled record of verified information, supporting evidence, approval history and open exceptions. Use Excel to identify what needs attention, then use documents and independent checks to make the decision.

Official references: GST Portal: Search Taxpayer manual and Microsoft Support: COUNTIF. This article is general information. Follow your organisation's vendor, data-security and tax-review policies.
