How to Investigate Bank Reconciliation Differences Step by Step

26 November 2026 · 6 min read

A bank reconciliation is useful only when the unmatched items are understood. A difference should not be cleared simply because two amounts look similar. The objective is to identify what happened, support the conclusion and record the right follow-up.

1. Start with a controlled comparison

Use the same statement period and the correct bank account and ledger account. Confirm the opening balance, closing balance and statement date before investigating individual lines. Keep an untouched copy of the source statement and ledger extract so the reconciliation can be reproduced.

Bring the two datasets into a consistent format. Dates should be real spreadsheet dates, amounts should be numeric, and transaction references should be stored as text so leading zeroes are not lost. Keep debit and credit direction consistent before comparing values.

2. Classify the difference before trying to clear it

  • Timing difference: an entry is recorded in the books but appears in the bank statement in a later period, or vice versa.
  • Missing book entry: the bank has processed a charge, receipt or transfer that has not yet been recorded in the ledger.
  • Duplicate entry: the same transaction may have been imported or posted more than once.
  • Amount or date mismatch: the reference may be correct while the recorded value or date is wrong.
  • Reversal or failed transaction: a debit may be followed by a reversal, refund or return entry.
  • Unidentified transaction: the statement description does not yet provide enough evidence to identify the counterparty or purpose.

These are investigation categories, not automatic explanations. Confirm each one against the statement, voucher, remittance advice, payment-gateway report or other supporting record.

3. Match using more than the amount

Where available, begin with a unique bank reference, UTR or transaction ID together with the amount and direction. If the reference is missing, compare date, amount, counterparty and narration, then inspect the supporting document. An amount-only match can incorrectly pair two unrelated transactions of the same value.

In Excel, XLOOKUP can retrieve a candidate record by a cleaned reference. COUNTIF or COUNTIFS can flag repeated references, while SUMIFS can help compare grouped totals by date, account or transaction type. Treat formulas as exception-finding aids. Review the underlying rows before marking an item as reconciled.

4. Maintain an exception register

For every unresolved line, record the statement date, transaction date, amount, bank reference, ledger voucher, exception type, owner, evidence checked, next action and target follow-up date. Use clear statuses such as Open, Under Review, Waiting for Support, Adjustment Proposed and Closed.

Assign one owner to each open item. A short note such as “bank fee confirmed in statement; voucher pending” is more useful than “difference to check”. Keep a link or reference to the evidence rather than relying on memory or an undocumented chat.

5. Post adjustments only after verification

If the investigation confirms a missing fee or an incorrect posting, prepare the appropriate accounting entry under the organisation's approval process. Check the account, tax treatment where relevant, period and supporting document. Do not create a journal merely to make the reconciliation total agree.

6. Review ageing and recurring causes

At close, review open items by age, amount, owner and reason. Repeated gateway fees, delayed settlements or duplicate imports may point to a process issue that should be corrected at source. Escalate material or long-outstanding items through the normal finance review process.

Practical rule

Every cleared item should have a defensible match or an approved adjustment. Every uncleared item should have an owner, an explanation of what is known and a next action.

Suggested Excel columns

Statement Date | Bank Reference | Narration | Debit | Credit | Book Voucher | Match Status | Exception Type | Owner | Evidence Reference | Next Action | Ageing

Use dummy or anonymised data when building a sample reconciliation workbook for training or a portfolio. Never publish customer bank details, account numbers, UTRs or employer records.
